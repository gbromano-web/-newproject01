How to Reheat Fried Chicken So It Stays Crispy (5 Ways That Work)

By the Manookan Fried Chicken House team · Updated October 15, 2026

The best way to reheat fried chicken so it stays crispy is dry, circulating heat: an air fryer at 180 °C for 4 to 6 minutes, or a turbo broiler or oven at 200 °C for 10 to 15 minutes. Moisture is the enemy of crunch, so the microwave on its own is the method most likely to leave you with soggy skin.

Below you'll find exact times and temperatures for five methods, how to store leftovers safely in hot weather, and the small mistakes that ruin a good crust.

Why fried chicken goes soggy (and how to stop it)

A fresh crust is crisp because frying drives water out of the coating. As chicken cools in a closed container, steam from the hot meat moves back into that coating and softens it.

Reheating works when it pushes that moisture out again. That means dry heat, air flowing around every side, and enough space between pieces for steam to escape.

Three rules that apply to every method

  • Rest it first. Let the chicken sit at room temperature for about 10 minutes so the middle heats through before the outside burns.
  • Don't crowd it. Leave a finger's width between pieces. Crowded chicken steams instead of crisping.
  • Lift it up. A rack or basket lets hot air reach the bottom. Chicken lying flat on a tray gets a soggy underside.

Method 1: How to reheat fried chicken in an air fryer (best)

An air fryer is a small convection oven, so it is the fastest way to bring back a fresh-fried crunch.

  1. Preheat the air fryer to 180 °C (350 °F) for 3 minutes.
  2. Place the pieces in a single layer in the basket, skin side up.
  3. Heat for 4 minutes, turn each piece, then heat 1 to 2 minutes more.
  4. Check that the thickest piece is hot all the way to the bone before serving.

Tip: skip the oil spray. Fried chicken already has enough fat in its coating, and extra oil can make it greasy.

Method 2: Reheat fried chicken in a turbo broiler

The turbo broiler, the glass-bowl convection cooker found in many Filipino kitchens, works almost as well as an air fryer and fits a whole family set at once.

  1. Set the turbo broiler to 200 °C and preheat for 5 minutes.
  2. Arrange the chicken on the upper rack in one layer, skin side up.
  3. Heat for 10 to 12 minutes. Turn the pieces halfway through if your broiler heats unevenly.
  4. Leave the lid slightly open for the last 2 minutes to let steam escape.

Method 3: Reheat fried chicken in the oven

A regular or toaster oven is the best choice for 6 pieces or more, like leftovers from a 12-piece barkada order.

  1. Preheat the oven to 200 °C (400 °F).
  2. Put a wire rack on a baking tray and arrange the chicken on the rack.
  3. Heat for 10 to 15 minutes. Wings and drumsticks finish first; breasts take longest.
  4. For extra crunch, switch to the top heating element only for the final 2 minutes, and watch it closely.

No rack? Crumple a sheet of foil into ridges and set the chicken on top. It lifts the pieces just enough.

Method 4: Reheat fried chicken in a pan

No oven or air fryer? A thick frying pan works well for a few pieces.

  1. Heat a dry pan over medium heat. Add no oil.
  2. Add the chicken skin side down and cover the pan with a lid.
  3. Cook 3 to 4 minutes, turn, cover again and cook another 3 to 4 minutes.
  4. Remove the lid for the last minute so trapped steam can escape.

The lid helps the inside heat through, and taking it off at the end protects the crust.

Method 5: Microwave fried chicken without making it soggy

The microwave heats the water inside the chicken, which turns to steam and softens the coating. You can still use it when time is short, as long as you finish in a pan.

  1. Place the chicken on a plate lined with a paper towel, uncovered.
  2. Microwave on high for 60 to 90 seconds per piece.
  3. Move it straight into a hot, dry pan for 1 minute per side to dry and re-crisp the crust.

How to store fried chicken safely in hot weather

Good reheating starts with good storage. Bacteria multiply fastest between about 5 °C and 60 °C, and a warm Cebu afternoon sits right in that range.

  • Refrigerate within 2 hours of buying. If it's very hot outside (above about 32 °C), make it 1 hour.
  • Let steam out first. Leave the container open for a few minutes before sealing, so condensation doesn't soak the crust.
  • Use a paper towel. Line the container with one to absorb moisture overnight.
  • Eat within 3 to 4 days from the fridge. To keep it longer, freeze it.

Can you freeze fried chicken?

Yes. Wrap each piece, store them in a freezer bag, and use within about 2 months for best texture. Thaw overnight in the fridge, then reheat with the air fryer, turbo broiler or oven method. Add 3 to 5 minutes to the usual time.

How to tell it's heated through

Reheated chicken should be steaming hot in the thickest part, next to the bone. With a food thermometer, look for at least 74 °C (165 °F). Without one, cut into the thickest piece: the meat should be hot all the way through, with no cold center.

5 mistakes that ruin a crispy crust

  • Covering it in the oven. Foil over the top traps steam. Leave it uncovered.
  • Reheating straight from the fridge at high heat. The outside burns before the inside warms. Rest it 10 minutes.
  • Sealing it while hot. Condensation is what makes leftovers soggy in the first place.
  • Reheating more than once. Each cycle dries the meat and raises food safety risk. Reheat only what you'll eat.
  • Adding sauce before reheating. Sauce softens the crust. Add gravy or dips after.

Key takeaways

  • Dry, circulating heat brings the crunch back: air fryer first, turbo broiler or oven second.
  • Rest chicken for 10 minutes, keep pieces apart, and lift them on a rack.
  • Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour on very hot days) and eat within 3 to 4 days.
  • Use the microwave only with a quick pan finish.
